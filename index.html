A companion archive to the NIMHANS Brain Museum, Bengaluru, where more than four hundred preserved brains have been kept since 1974.

A label that says what, never who.

Each specimen on those shelves is catalogued by its pathology. The person's name, the family's account, and the conditions that shaped the illness are not part of the record.

This archive collects that second account — from caregivers, clinicians, and the public in Karnataka — and keeps it under consent the contributor can still change.

Every label returns the same blanks

These are labels observed on the shelves. Choose one and watch what changes. The diagnosis is the only field that moves; the six blanks below it stay blank, because a collection built to preserve tissue has nowhere to put a biography.

The label above the blanks now reads neurocysticercosis.

Two of the museum's seven zones hold brain specimens. Specimens that degrade past use are removed and destroyed — nothing here is kept forever, and the museum makes no claim that it is.

Plates

Photographs from the visit, and room for whatever else belongs here. Empty frames stay visible rather than being removed — an archive that shows its gaps is more honest than one that closes them.

The second account

Thirteen people have contributed so far, through an open questionnaire. Twelve appear below in their own words. One is held back. Records are arranged by who is speaking and under what consent — never by diagnosis.

Who can see what

Not a public-or-private switch. Access follows the relationship a person has to the material, and can be renegotiated after a recording already exists.

Public

Composite or anonymised accounts, edited with the contributor's approval to remove identifying detail. Open to any visitor. This page is the public tier.

Family

Fuller, named accounts visible only to the contributing family and anyone they authorise. Material can move outward later, never automatically.

Research

Available to accredited researchers under an ethics agreement a community advisory board can revoke.

Consent to donate a brain is not consent to have a life described. Participation here is a separate agreement, opt-in, open to anyone connected to psychiatric or neurological illness whether or not tissue is involved — and it can be narrowed or withdrawn at any point, including after a recording exists. Each entry carries a review date at which its tier is reconsidered.

An advisory board of former patients, family members, and caregivers — not only clinicians and archivists — would hold standing authority to restrict or remove contributed material.

Notes on this archive

What this is

A student research project by Monosiz Xavier Mallick (7BAENG, 2333151) for Memory Machines: The Politics of Digital Preservation, ENG405A-7B, supervised by Prof. Renu Elizabeth Abraham. It is a proposal and a working prototype, not a funded or institutionally hosted archive.

What it isn't

Not a correction to the NIMHANS collection and not a replacement for it. A diagnosis-first collection isn't wrong to exist; it is structurally unable to hold this material.

What's missing

  • Contributions in Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, and Hindi. Every response so far is in English, which excludes the caregivers whose accounts are most absent from the existing record.
  • Audio and video oral histories. Present contributions are written questionnaire responses only.
  • One caregiver account, withheld pending confirmation of its tier consent.
  • A named community advisory board.
  • A host. The proposal calls for an open-source, community-governed platform — a Mukurtu instance run by a university archival studies programme or a Karnataka mental health NGO — so authority stays near the community rather than the servers.
